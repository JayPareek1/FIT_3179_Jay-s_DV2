Growing Up Ready

Almost every child now starts preschool. So why do so many still miss out on the full benefit?

By [Your name] · October 2026

Nearly every child is enrolled

92out of 100

In 2025, about 92 in every 100 children in the year before school were enrolled in a preschool program. That is a year of play-based learning led by a qualified teacher.

Enrolment has been this high for ten years (91 in 100 in 2016). So the real questions are where children learn, how much they attend and who still misses out.

Same year, different settings

A preschool program can run in a preschool, often at a school or community centre. It can also run inside a day care centre.

Which one a family finds depends on the state. In Western Australia, two in three four-year-olds learn in a preschool. In Queensland, seven in ten do their program in a day care centre.

Enrolment depends on where you live

South Australia and the Northern Territory had the lowest enrolment in 2025, at 86 in every 100 children. New South Wales was close behind at 87.

Western Australia, Tasmania and the ACT are all above 100.

Why above 100? Enrolment counts and population estimates come from different sources. Some children also enrol in another state. So a number just over 100 means “almost every child”.

Enrolled does not mean attending enough

The national benchmark is 600 hours of preschool in the year before school. That is about 15 hours a week across a school year.

Across Australia, about 72 in every 100 enrolled children reach it. In South Australia, Western Australia and the Northern Territory it is closer to 6 in 10. In Tasmania and New South Wales it is above 8 in 10.

How we estimated this. The dark dot is the share enrolled multiplied by the share of enrolled children who attend 600 or more hours. It is our estimate, not an official count.
