Growing Up Ready

Almost every child now starts preschool. So why do so many still miss out on the full benefit?

By Jay Pareek · October 2026 · GitHub

Nearly every child is enrolled

92out of 100

In 2025, about 92 in every 100 children in the year before school were enrolled in a preschool program: a year of play-based learning with a qualified teacher.

Enrolment has stayed between 87 and 92 in every 100 since 2018. So the questions that matter now are:

  • Where do children learn?
  • How much do they attend?
  • Who is still missing out?

Same year, different settings

A preschool program can run in a preschool, often at a school or community centre, or inside a day care centre.

It depends on the state. Western Australia has the highest share in preschools: two in three four-year-olds. Queensland has the highest share in day care centres: seven in ten.

Enrolment depends on where you live

The Northern Territory and South Australia had the lowest enrolment in 2025: 86 in every 100 children. Western Australia, Tasmania and the ACT are above 100.

Enrolment does not mean high attendance

The national benchmark is 600 hours of preschool a year, about 15 hours a week.

Across Australia, about 72 in every 100 enrolled children reach it. It is lowest in South Australia, the Northern Territory and Western Australia (closer to 6 in 10) and highest in Tasmania and New South Wales (above 8 in 10).

Sources: Productivity Commission, Report on Government Services 2026, Table 3A.11; ABS, Preschool Attendance 2025, Table 1.

Services cluster where people live

Long day care centres and preschools offer about 870,000 approved places in around 12,700 services. Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland hold about four in five.

The biggest circles are in outer Melbourne and south-east Queensland. Across the centre and the north there are only a few small dots, because far fewer people live there.

What families pay

Across Australia, 29 in every 100 enrolled children pay nothing per hour. Most of the rest pay $1 to $9 an hour. Only about 2 in 100 pay $15 or more.

It varies by state: from more than half in the Northern Territory (56 in every 100) to 13 in every 100 in New South Wales.

Different groups, different starts

First Nations children

In 2018, about 70 in every 100 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children in the year before school were enrolled. By 2025 it was 101.

Victoria rose the most (by 64 in every 100). The Northern Territory rose the least (by 8) and had the lowest rate in 2025 (83).

Source: Productivity Commission, Report on Government Services 2026, Table 3A.19 (enrolled First Nations children divided by the estimated number of First Nations children in the year before school).

Note: Some values are above 100 because the enrolment counts and the population estimates come from different sources.

Neighbourhood disadvantage

Where preschool happens

Next, we zoom in on local communities.

Zooming in on local communities

Teachers complete the Australian Early Development Census for children in their first year of full-time school. A child is developmentally vulnerable if they score in the lowest 10% of 2009 results in at least one of five areas: physical health, social skills, emotional maturity, language and thinking, and communication.

Each hexagon covers about the same area, so a big outback region does not outweigh a city. Hexagons with 30% or more of children vulnerable fell from 69 in 2009 to 50 in 2015, then rose to 64 in 2024.

Those at 40% or more are mostly in Queensland, the Northern Territory and Western Australia. The highest values are in remote hexagons with few children assessed, so they can change a lot between years.

About this page

This page looks at early childhood education in the year before full-time school: how many children are enrolled, how much they attend, where services are, what they cost, and how children are doing when they start school. It is written for a general Australian reader.

Some numbers are our own calculations from the published counts: shares and rates, the 600-hour estimate (the enrolment rate multiplied by the share of enrolled children who attend 600 or more hours), the postcode and hexagon groupings, and the ranks.
