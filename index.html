Growing Up Ready

Almost every child now starts preschool. So why do so many still miss out on the full benefit?

By Jay Pareek

Nearly every child is enrolled

92out of 100

In 2025, about 92 in every 100 children in the year before school were enrolled in a preschool program. That is a year of play-based learning led by a qualified teacher.

Since 2018, between 87 and 92 in every 100 children have been enrolled each year. So the real questions are where children learn, how much they attend and who still misses out.

Same year, different settings

A preschool program can run in a preschool, often at a school or community centre. It can also run inside a day care centre.

Which one a family finds depends on the state. In Western Australia, two in three four-year-olds learn in a preschool. In Queensland, seven in ten do their program in a day care centre.

Enrolment depends on where you live

The Northern Territory had the lowest enrolment in 2025, at 86 in every 100 children. New South Wales was next at 87. South Australia also shows 86, but that figure is probably too low.

Western Australia, Tasmania and the ACT are all above 100.

How the rate works. Enrolled children are divided by an estimate of how many children are that age. Estimates are never exact, so a rate can pass 100. Read a number just over 100 as “almost every child”.

Why is South Australia probably too low? It now lets children start school mid-year. The estimate does not allow for this, so it counts too many children and the rate comes out too low.

Enrolment does not mean high attendance

The national benchmark is 600 hours of preschool in the year before school. That is about 15 hours a week across a school year.

Across Australia, about 72 in every 100 enrolled children reach it. In South Australia, Western Australia and the Northern Territory it is closer to 6 in 10. In Tasmania and New South Wales it is above 8 in 10.

How we estimated this. The dark dot is the share enrolled multiplied by the share of enrolled children who attend 600 or more hours. It is our estimate, not an official count. South Australia’s dots are probably too low (see the map notes).

Services cluster where people live

Across Australia, long day care centres and preschools offer about 870,000 approved places in around 12,700 services. Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland hold about four in five of them.

The biggest circles are in outer Melbourne and south-east Queensland. Across the centre and the north there are only a few small dots, because far fewer people live there. Families in those areas may have to travel further to reach a service.

Not a full count. The register lists services approved under the national quality system. Some preschools, such as those inside schools, may not appear. So the counts may be low in states where schools run most preschools.
